## v1.44.0 — SAFETY: a manifest entry can no longer close the manifest fence (FB-0109)

**2026-09-17.** Breaking changes: none.

**If you have an open PR that flow marked ready under v1.43.0 or earlier, re-check it.** This
release fixes a bug that could make a pull request with a *failed* behavioural gate read as
merge-ready. Run `/flow:doctor` against any such PR before merging it.

- **A manifest entry could erase the rest of the manifest.** `/flow:ship`'s "🚫 NOT READY TO
  MERGE" block is delimited by an HTML-comment fence. The parser found the closing fence as a
  bare substring — the first occurrence anywhere in the body — so an entry whose *finding text*
  contained that marker ended the region at itself and every entry after it vanished. Measured on
  the pre-fix tree: a body carrying one marker-bearing entry plus a real `[verify-build]` blocker
  parsed with the blocker **absent**, and the verdict moved `DECIDE` → `READY`. Reachability was a
  latent self-trigger, one docs commit from live: `[status-surface]` findings quote verbatim lines
  out of your repo's own orientation docs.

- **Fixed in two layers that deliberately want opposite rules.** Finding the fences uses the
  *narrowest* line definition (line-anchored, `\n` only, ending at the **last** close) — fewer
  things counting as a fence means a wider region and more blockers surviving. Reading the entries
  uses the *widest* (the union of `str.splitlines()` and `split("\n")`) — more things counting as
  a line means more blockers found. Applying one rule to both looks consistent and is backwards;
  measured, each single split erases a live blocker in the shape the other one handles.

- **A second forgery, found by this release's own security review.** The manifest line's field
  separators are neutralized at write time so a quoted one cannot forge a field — but that defang
  matched *literal strings* while the parser matches a *whitespace class*. A finding carrying a TAB
  or a non-breaking space before the em dash matched the parser and missed the defang. Measured: a
  `[security]` finding needing `secret rotation` — normally **blocked and not waivable** — became
  `ask` and one-word-waivable. Now matched with the parser's own grammar. Honest prose discussing a
  resolution verb still ships and still reads normally.

- **The block you read is more honest about what it doesn't know.** Because the fence scan
  deliberately degrades toward *more* entries, it can sweep ordinary prose into the manifest. Such
  an entry no longer claims "a ship gate did not pass" — it says it may be quoted text, offers
  "it's prose, not a blocker — drop the line", and attributes any proposal in it to the line rather
  than presenting it as flow's own recommendation.

**Known gaps, stated plainly.** Line anchoring alone does not reach a finding embedding a real
newline before a bare marker; for text written through `add-entry` that is closed by v1.42.0's
newline collapse, so the remaining exposure is a PR body assembled *outside* `add-entry` (hand-edited
on GitHub). `pr-coherence.has_manifest()` still matches its tokens unanchored — failing toward a
wedged ship rather than a bypass, and defanged write-side by v1.42.0. A fifth structural token
(`already-attempted`) is measured, fail-safe, and left open on the roadmap.

Regression cover: a `[fence-injection]` section and a `P23` section in
`run_manifest_triage_evals.py`, mutation-tested against seven builds (21 / 8 / 8 / 9 / 1 / 1 / 0
failures; 0 on the fix). Full detail, including the two refutations this fix survived and the two
times its own eval failed to catch its own bug, is in
`dev-docs/history/2026-09-13-safety-a-manifest-entry-can-no-longer-close-the-manifest-fence.md`.

FB-0109.
