## v1.44.0 — SAFETY: a manifest entry can no longer close the manifest fence (FB-0109)

**2026-09-17.** Breaking changes: none.

`/flow:ship`'s NOT-READY manifest is delimited by an HTML-comment fence.
`extract_manifest_region` located the closing fence as a bare substring — the
first occurrence anywhere in the PR body — so an entry whose *finding text*
contained that marker truncated the region at itself and every entry after it
vanished from the parse.

Measured on the pre-fix tree: a body carrying one marker-bearing entry followed
by a real `[verify-build]` blocker parsed with the verify-build blocker
**absent**, and the coherence verdict moved `DECIDE` → `READY`. A pull request
whose behavioural gate had failed read as merge-ready.

**The fix, in two layers that want opposite rules.**

- **Finding the fences** (`manifest_contract.extract_manifest_region`): both
  fences are matched **line-anchored**, splitting on `\n` only (never
  `str.splitlines()`), and the region ends at the **last** line-anchored close
  rather than the first. Here the *narrowest* line definition is the safe one —
  the fewer things count as a fence, the wider the region, the more entries
  survive.
- **Reading the entries** (`manifest-triage.parse_entries`): the region is split
  by the **union** of `str.splitlines()` and `split("\n")`, deduped by
  fingerprint. Here the *widest* definition is the safe one — the more things
  count as a line, the more candidate blockers are found.

Applying one rule to both layers looks consistent and is backwards. That mistake
was made twice during this change, and caught both times by review rather than by
the author:

- `str.splitlines()` breaks on **eight** further code points (`\x0b \x0c \x1c
  \x1d \x1e \x85    `). A finding carrying any of them around a bare
  marker still erased the `[verify-build]` blocker — measured, all eight.
- **Last close, not first.** A doc-style example quoting both markers on their
  own lines *above* the real manifest captured the region under first-close, and
  the real entries vanished — fewer entries, the unsafe direction.
- **Then `split("\n")` alone broke the layer below.** `parse_entries` consumes
  the region and re-splits it. Under `splitlines()`, an entry whose *own* finding
  text carried one of the eight was fragmented and silently dropped. Under
  `split("\n")`, two entries *joined* by one of them were read as a single
  physical line, so the first swallowed the second — blocker and all. Worse than
  a drop: the survivor absorbs the victim's ` — needs:` verb, and `needs` is the
  field `classify()` derives an entry's class and waivability from, so a dropped
  blocker also forges the field that decides whether a human ever sees it.
  Neither split is safe alone; the union is a superset of both by construction.

**A second forgery, found by this PR's own security review and fixed here.** The manifest line's
field separators (` — needs:`, ` — confidence:`, ` — candidate resolutions:`) are neutralized at write
time so a finding quoting one cannot forge the field. That defang matched **literal strings**; the
parser matches a **whitespace class** (`\s+—\s*needs:`). So a finding carrying a TAB or a
non-breaking space before the em dash matched the parser and missed the defang.

Measured: a `[security]` finding written with `--needs "secret rotation"` — an out-of-session verb,
so classified `blocked` and **not waivable** — parsed as `needs: design decision`, class `ask`,
`waivable: true`, and the verdict moved `BLOCKED` → `DECIDE`. A leaked-secret item became
one-word-waivable from bytes the finding text supplied. Two spaces happened to be safe only because
the one-space literal is a substring of them, which is what made the literal look correct.

The separators are now matched with the parser's own grammar. Widening the match defangs *more*
prose, never less, and defanging (not refusing) remains the behaviour, so a finding that legitimately
discusses a resolution verb still ships and still reads normally.

The rule this generalizes to, recorded in FB-0109: **a defense must match on the same grammar its
consumer parses with.** A literal guarding a regex is not a guard, it is a sample of one.

**What this does not fix, stated plainly.** Line anchoring alone does not reach a
finding that embeds a real `\n` before a bare marker. For text written through
`add-entry`, that is already closed at write time by the newline collapse and
marker defang shipped in **v1.42.0** — verified end to end against this tree, not
inferred from a sibling branch. The honest remaining gap is a body that did *not*
come through `add-entry`: hand-edited on GitHub, or assembled from sections the
write guard never touched. There, line anchoring is the only layer.

**A third structural token exists and this change does not reach it.**
`pr-coherence.py`'s `has_manifest()` is an unanchored substring test on both the
fence marker and the `🚫 NOT READY TO MERGE` heading. Its failure direction is the
opposite of this one's: not a bypass but a **wedge** — a body that merely mentions
the sentinel makes a READY PR read as carrying a manifest, halting a clean ship.
It is defanged write-side by v1.42.0 and remains owned there.

**Failure direction:** if the fences are not found line-anchored, the parser
returns the whole body — the same as "fences absent" — so it sees more candidate
entries, never fewer. A merge gate degrades toward not-ready. Because that
widening can sweep ordinary prose into the block a human reads, an entry whose
kind flow does not recognize no longer claims "A ship gate did not pass"; it says
it may be quoted text rather than a real blocker, and asks for a look.

Reachability: a latent self-trigger, one docs commit from live. `[status-surface]`
findings quote a verbatim line from a scanned candidate; none of the six defaults
carries the marker today, but `README.md` already discusses the manifest.

Regression cover: `run_manifest_triage_evals.py` gains a `[fence-injection]`
section — the attack, a prose quote of both markers, a control, the fail-safe
direction, all eight separators around the marker, all eight *inside* an entry,
all eight *joining* two entries, the preceding and trailing fence-pair cases, an
unclosed fence, a CRLF body driven against the engine directly, an emitter↔reader
round trip, and a `P23` section driving the forged separator through eight
whitespace shapes to its classification consequence — plus paired positives
asserting that fence scoping, recognized-kind copy, and honest verb-discussing
prose all still work (without which "delete it" or "defang everything" would
satisfy every other assertion).

Mutation-tested against seven builds, measured against the final eval: **21**
failures on the pre-fix extractor, **8** with the wide split alone, **8** with the
narrow split alone, **9** with the literal field-separator defang restored, **1**
with first-close instead of last-close, **1** with the CRLF normalization removed,
and **0** on the fix. The
separator cases deliberately use a body with **no real close fence** — with one
present, the last-close rule rescues the entry independently, so a closed-fence
body passes even under `splitlines()` and would not isolate the property it
names. The first revision of this eval made exactly that mistake and went green
against the broken build.

FB-0109.
